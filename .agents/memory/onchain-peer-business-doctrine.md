---
name: On-chain peer business doctrine
description: Product direction for lending and commerce: members should transact directly with machine-enforced rules, not be routed into traditional gatekept credit structures.
---

CryptoOwnBank's preferred destination is **on-chain peer-to-peer business**: members choose counterparties and terms, approve their own transactions, and rely on transparent protocol rules to hold collateral, release funds, collect repayment, or resolve a predefined outcome. Trust formation is deliberately off-chain and member-led: people may use a phone call, email, introduction, or an existing relationship to decide whom to do business with. COB does not score, manufacture, or police that trust.

Institutional "on-chain" products that retain fund managers, borrower sourcing, credit underwriting, eligibility gates, or discretionary loss handling are not the target end state. They may be market intelligence or an optional external research reference, but must never be framed as the realization of the sovereignty mission.

**Why:** putting settlement on a blockchain does not remove a middleman if humans still decide who may participate, allocate capital, or intervene in the credit relationship. The founder's concern is member agency — two people who agree to terms should be able to make a neutral smart contract enforce them, just as if they met in person and shook hands, without COB becoming a trust broker.

**How to apply:** evaluate lending, escrow, marketplace, and payment features by asking whether the members can inspect terms, choose each other, sign, and exit directly. Favor transparent, overcollateralized or otherwise objectively enforceable protocols. Treat unsecured credit as a separate trust/reputation problem that members may solve off-chain; COB must not silently become the judge, scorer, or gatekeeper merely because the final agreement settles on-chain.
