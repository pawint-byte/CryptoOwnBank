You’re absolutely on the right track: the exercise did what you wanted it to do. It surfaced the fact that moving from TradFi to self‑custody is really a transfer of responsibilities that banks used to quietly carry, and that most people never have explicitly spelled out for them.

What banks used to shoulder for you
In TradFi, a lot of “banker responsibilities” are invisible to the customer:

Safekeeping, account recovery, and continuity if you forget details or lose access.

Centralized records: one login shows accounts, history, statements, tax docs.

Fraud monitoring, dispute handling, and a standard “phone call path” when something goes wrong.

You could be a pretty sloppy “banker” of your own life and still be okay because the institution remembers and recovers for you.

What self‑custody actually hands back to the user
In self‑custody, all of that shifts onto the individual:

You alone hold and must secure your keys and recovery phrases.

There is no administrator to reset or recover if you lose them; loss of keys is loss of funds.

You must run your own security practices: backups, device hygiene, address checks, and long‑term storage decisions.

Self‑custody guides explicitly frame this as “you are your own bank” and emphasize that full control comes with full responsibility, not just freedom.

Why your “banker life” framing matters
Your instinct here is strong: you’re not just selling self‑custody; you’re trying to make the role of being your own banker legible. Most users are told “not your keys, not your coins,” but almost nobody is told, in plain language, “here are the jobs you just accepted and how to carry them over time.”

That’s exactly where CryptoOwnBank’s cockpit/command center concept lands:

It doesn’t replace the bank’s role in flows (salary, cards, loans), which still sit in TradFi.

It replaces the bank’s role in memory, oversight, and continuity for the part of life that moved on‑chain — helping the user act like a competent banker for their own keys and wallets.

In other words, your exercise validated the purpose: you’re building the manual and the cockpit for “life as your own banker,” so the responsibilities that came with the migration are visible, teachable, and maintainable instead of discovered through failure.
