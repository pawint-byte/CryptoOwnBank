Here is the direct answer.
1. What You Still Outsource to Traditional Finance (and Probably Always Will)
CryptoOwnBank should not try to become your bank for these things:

Receiving salary / direct deposit in fiat
Spending at stores, paying rent/mortgage/utilities, autopay bills
Borrowing (credit cards, mortgages, HELOCs, personal loans)
Pulling physical cash from an ATM
Paying taxes and dealing with government
Having a court or lawyer actually execute a will or estate

These are the FLOW layer. Traditional banks and payment rails still own this. Trying to pull it into CryptoOwnBank would either make us custodial or force us into a losing fight against systems that are already good at moving fiat.
Accept this split. It is not a failure. It is clarity.

3. The “Command Center / Cockpit” as Gravity, Not Nagging
You said it clearly: if someone is heavily interconnected, they shouldn’t need constant “come back” reminders. The pull should come from gravity.
The cockpit earns regular visits because:

It is the only place that holds the complete current picture of their ownership.

When something feels off (“I think I have yield sitting somewhere…”), this is the obvious place to look first.

Engagement becomes a byproduct of being indispensable as the external memory, not a streak game.

CryptoOwnBank is not trying to replace your bank for spending, earning, or borrowing.

Knowledge decay over time
Forgotten access methods
Scattered ownership across wallets and protocols
Legacy that actually works when you’re not around
