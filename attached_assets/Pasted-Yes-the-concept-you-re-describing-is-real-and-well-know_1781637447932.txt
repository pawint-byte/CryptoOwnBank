Yes — the concept you're describing is real and well-known.
It's called a ROSCA (Rotating Savings and Credit Association).
In the Caribbean it goes by different local names:

Jamaica: Partner or Pardna
Trinidad, Grenada, etc.: Sou-Sou or Susu
Guyana and some other places: Box Hand or Meeting Turn

The basic idea is exactly what you said: a group pools fixed contributions on a regular schedule (often weekly), and each period one member receives the full pot ("gets their hand"). It rotates until everyone has received theirs. It's a powerful, trust-based mix of forced savings + interest-free credit that has existed for generations, especially in communities where traditional banks are expensive, inaccessible, or culturally distant.

Why This Is Interesting On-Chain
Traditional ROSCAs work extremely well because of social pressure and trust within tight communities. The blockchain version can add:

Transparency — Everyone can see contributions and the rotation schedule on-chain.
Automation — Smart contracts enforce the payout order and timing (no one can "forget" or argue about whose turn it is).
Remote participation — Family in Jamaica, US, UK, Canada can all be in the same hand without physical meetings.
Yield on the pot — While money sits waiting for someone’s hand, the pool could earn DeFi yield (e.g., stablecoin lending on Morpho/Aave or XRPL-native options).
Composability — Tie it into other tools (e.g., private family groups in FamilyRoots.family + on-chain execution).
Lower disputes — Immutable record of who paid and when.

Challenges that still exist:

Group formation and trust — Blockchain doesn’t magically create trust between strangers. Most successful versions will probably still start with real-world relationships (family, church, community groups).
Default risk — If someone stops paying after they received their hand, enforcement is harder than pure social pressure (though smart contracts + collateral or reputation layers can help).
Onboarding and UX — Still clunky for many retail users.
Regulatory gray areas in some jurisdictions.

Opportunity Angle (Tying to What You're Building)
This feels like a natural extension of the self-sovereignty thesis we’ve been discussing.
Traditional banks don’t really offer this product at scale (it’s too relationship-driven and low-margin for them). Fintechs sometimes try (e.g., digital sou-sou apps in various countries), but they usually add fees and take custody.
An on-chain version could let communities run their own hands with:

Stablecoins for stability (USDC, RLUSD, etc.)
Optional yield on the pooled funds
Private or semi-private groups
Integration with legacy/family tools

It directly addresses the “life as your own banker” responsibilities we talked about earlier — but in a community-powered way instead of pure individual self-custody.
