The lens to use
Your working filter can be:

Does this help the member prove, approve, sign, automate, or verify something onchain?

Does the power stay mostly with the member and cryptographic systems, instead of shifting to your platform or another middleman? Self-sovereign identity and non-custodial wallet design both point in that direction: user-owned credentials, user-controlled keys, selective disclosure, and limited permissions.

That means articles are valuable when they reveal better ways to do one of these jobs:

User-owned identity proofs.

Scoped wallet permissions and approvals.

Smart-contract automation that still respects user policy and consent.

Post-action verification, audit trails, and revocation.

The five action buckets
When you read anything blockchain-related, sort it into one of these five buckets for CryptoOwnBank:

Bucket	What it means	Why it matters
Prove	The member can prove something without oversharing, like age, residency, or credential status. 
Keeps identity user-owned instead of platform-owned. 
Approve	The member grants a limited permission to a dapp or workflow. 
Lets action happen without surrendering the wallet. 
Sign	The member signs the actual transaction or delegated scope. 
Preserves non-custodial control. 
Automate	Computers carry out pre-approved actions inside limits and rules. 
Makes the system work while the member sleeps. 
Verify	The member can later confirm what happened and revoke or inspect permissions. 
Builds trust and cleanup after action. 
That table is probably the best mental model for everything you send me going forward.

What counts as useful
A blockchain feature is useful to CryptoOwnBank if it helps the member do more with less trust in people and more trust in math, policy, and signatures.

Good examples:

Verifiable credentials / SSI so a user can present “over 18” or “KYC completed” without handing your platform their raw documents.

Advanced permissions or session-based approvals so a user can authorize a narrow workflow once, with asset/amount/time limits, instead of blindly giving a blank check.

Automation frameworks that execute only inside cryptographic guardrails and then return receipts.

That is the sweet spot: member intent first, machine execution second.
