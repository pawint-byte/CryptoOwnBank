A traditional bank mostly offers safekeeping, record‑keeping, payments, credit, and a “managed experience,” while being your own bank replaces those services with direct control, direct responsibility, and new behaviors around custody, security, and planning.

I’ll break that into: what a bank actually does for you, and what changes when you do those functions yourself.

What a traditional bank does
Underneath the marketing, banks do a few core jobs for customers:

Safekeeping and custody. They hold your deposits and other assets in accounts, keep records, reconcile balances, and protect them with their security stack and balance sheet.

Payments and access. They give you rails to move money: debit cards, wires, ACH, bill pay, checks, P2P transfers, and sometimes FX and cross‑border.

Credit and leverage. They underwrite you and extend credit (credit cards, overdrafts, personal and business loans, mortgages) so you can use more money than you currently hold.

Intermediation and maturity transformation. They pool deposits and lend them out; you get stability and convenience, they manage liquidity, interest rate risk, and capital requirements.

Risk and compliance shielding. They carry a lot of regulatory and operational weight: KYC/AML, fraud monitoring, dispute resolution, FDIC/insurance frameworks, and formal complaints channels.

Advice and customer service. Branches, call centers, relationship managers – humans who help you fix mistakes, reset access, replace cards, and navigate products.

Day‑to‑day, this looks like: log into app, see balances, pay bills, swipe card, maybe talk to support. The bank abstracts away the machinery, and you operate at a high level.

What “being your own bank” actually means
In crypto/self‑custody terms, “be your own bank” basically means: you take over many of those roles a bank plays, especially custody and transaction authorization.

Key characteristics:

You hold the keys. Self‑custody means you directly control the private keys to your assets; no intermediary can move your funds or freeze them.

No counterparty risk (but more self‑risk). Your assets can’t be lost in an exchange failure or bank insolvency, but they can be lost via your own mistakes, scams, or mis‑backed seed phrase.

24/7 borderless access. You can move value globally, at any time, without asking permission, as long as you can sign a transaction.

Higher privacy and censorship‑resistance. There’s no centralized account to freeze or surveil in the same way; control is on‑chain via keys.

Operational burden shifts to you. You become responsible for security practices, backups, transaction review, and avoiding malicious contracts or addresses.

So the value prop is: sovereignty and independence instead of convenience and delegation.
