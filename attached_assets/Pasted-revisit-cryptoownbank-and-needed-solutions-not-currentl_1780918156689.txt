4. Comprehensive Analysis
High-Confidence Findings
All three models converge on the same governing constraint: CryptoOwnBank’s safest lane is being a software interface that never takes custody, never controls keys, and never routes funds through platform-controlled accounts. That matches the public positioning of CryptoOwnBank as a non-custodial dashboard. Practically, this means every “bank-like” feature must be implemented as either (a) read-only analytics, or (b) a transaction builder where the member’s wallet signs and sends transactions directly to onchain contracts.

There is also strong convergence that the highest-leverage upgrade is account abstraction / smart-wallet UX. Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5 emphasize it as foundational; Gemini agrees it yields bank-like safety (recovery) and convenience (gas handling) without custody. Account abstraction is specifically associated with batching, recovery, and gas sponsorship mechanics. In addition, EIP-7702 + paymaster patterns can let users pay gas with USDC, removing the “must hold ETH for gas” friction that blocks mainstream adoption. If you want “maximize what is available onchain” while staying out of compliance trouble, AA is the cleanest multiplier because it improves UX across every other module (yield, inheritance actions, payments, revokes).

Finally, GPT-5.5 and Claude strongly align on a key product/legal lesson: do not market yield as “fixed APR” or “guaranteed,” and do not structure anything resembling pooled “earn accounts.” The SEC has historically targeted retail yield programs framed as interest-bearing products (Gemini Earn) and staking-as-a-service models where customers transfer assets for advertised returns. Even if enforcement posture evolves over time, your risk is driven by product design + marketing language. The safer pattern is “member connects wallet → chooses protocol → signs transaction → protocol pays whatever variable rate exists,” with clear risk disclosures.

Areas of Divergence
The main divergence is how aggressively to extend the product into “payments.” GPT-5.5 and Claude Opus 4.8 both want the convenience of onramps and merchant spending but insist it should be done via partners—e.g., an onramp component like Coinbase’s FundCard UI (where Coinbase is the onramp provider) and a payments network integration like WalletConnect Pay. Their implicit assumption is that members demand fiat adjacency (buying crypto, spending stablecoins) and that you can preserve your non-custodial posture by delegating regulated parts to third parties.

Gemini 3.1 takes a purist onchain route: instead of cards/onramps, it prioritizes “bill pay” via streaming payments, which can be implemented as pure smart-contract interaction (recurring token streams/allowances) without any fiat rails. This is lower compliance surface area but may not satisfy members who want a direct bridge to everyday commerce.

A second divergence is the “frontier features” layer. Gemini proposes ZK identity/reputation and P2P escrow. These can be powerful differentiators, but they introduce more complexity (new trust assumptions, more contracts, more user education). GPT-5.5 and Claude focus on proven primitives that already have mature tooling and user demand: Aave/Yearn integrations for user-directed DeFi actions, plus wallet safety tooling (approvals/revokes) powered by established data providers.

Unique Insights Worth Noting
Gemini’s “onchain bill pay” idea is unusually aligned with your stated goal (“maximize what is available onchain” while avoiding compliance exposure). A streaming/recurring-payment UI can be framed as: “You authorize a smart contract allowance/stream from your wallet; CryptoOwnBank never touches funds.” That’s consistent with your non-custodial posture. Even if you don’t ship full streaming on day one, a simpler first step is “recurring transactions” using AA batching + scheduled reminders, then expand to true streaming.

The P2P escrow generator is also potentially compelling if you keep it strictly as software templates and avoid acting as a broker/matching engine. It could solve a real member need (safe OTC swaps) while preserving “user signs everything.”

Recommendations
Ship in this order: (1) Account abstraction smart-wallet layer (recovery, spending rules, gas-in-USDC UX). (2) Wallet security center: approvals/allowances scan + revoke transaction builder. (3) DeFi access modules (Aave supply/withdraw/borrow; Yearn vault discovery) implemented as direct wallet-to-protocol transactions, with strict non-guarantee language. (4) Add either partner-based payments/onramp (Coinbase FundCard / WalletConnect Pay) for mainstream utility, or the purely onchain “bill pay” roadmap (recurring/streaming payments) if you want to stay maximally compliance-light.
