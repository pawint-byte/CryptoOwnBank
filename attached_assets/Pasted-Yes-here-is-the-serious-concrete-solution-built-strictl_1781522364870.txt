This is where the rubber meets the road.
Getting paid (example flow for digital services)

Price in USD equivalent but quote and accept primarily in stablecoins (RLUSD on XRPL or USDC on L2). Offer a modest discount for crypto payment to incentivize it.

Clear USD reference amount + crypto equivalent at time of invoice (or fixed crypto amount).
Multiple QR codes / addresses: RLUSD/XRP on XRPL (speed + your ecosystem), USDC on low-fee L2 (broad compatibility).
Invoice number in memo field where supported.
Payment instructions and “crypto preferred” framing.

Funds land in your hot wallet under your full control within seconds to minutes. Irreversible final settlement. No processor can claw it back or freeze it.

Paying others globally (vendors, contractors, products)

Crypto-native counterparties: Direct wallet-to-wallet stablecoin transfer. Instant, final, low cost. Build a preferred vendor list inside SolveForge or your internal tools.
Traditional suppliers: JIT conversion (small amounts only) via your thin interface or self-hosted ramp. Never leave material balances sitting in banks.
Physical products: Where the merchant accepts crypto directly — do so. Otherwise use established crypto gift card / on-ramp services or convert JIT. Shipping/customs will always carry some governmental touchpoints; the payment rail itself stays sovereign.

Record keeping & compliance (you own the data graph)

Maintain a private internal ledger you control (simple self-hosted database, or module you build into FamilyRoots/CryptoOwnBank). Map: Invoice ID → Client → USD equivalent → Crypto received (chain + tx hash + amount) → Date → Category.
On-chain data is public, but the mapping and business context stay private to you.
For taxes: Export categorized data at year-end. Use or build a lightweight importer. You report honestly — this is the unavoidable governmental touchpoint. Stablecoin holdings that don’t fluctuate have minimal gain/loss complexity.
No third-party payment processor owns or aggregates your full transaction graph.
