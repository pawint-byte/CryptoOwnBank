You’re not inventing the activities. You’re processizing them — giving them clean, repeatable structure so people stop fumbling with notebooks, WhatsApp threads, and “whose turn was it again?”
The Bright Line (Non-Custodial by Design)
This stays clean as long as you hold this line:

Members form the group and decide the rules offline (who’s in, what the rotation/order is, what the cause is). CryptoOwnBank never vets or approves participants or causes.
Each member signs their own actions with their own keys (contributions, approvals). Nothing moves because CryptoOwnBank pushed a button.
The smart contract + cockpit only does three things: enforces the agreed rule, moves the funds according to that rule, and shows everyone the transparent truth.
You never hold the pot. Funds move member-to-member (or to the designated recipient). You are the table, the schedule, and the shared ledger — not the bank.

This is exactly the posture that makes Legacy Plan work. Extending it to groups is natural.
Concrete Example: The Rotating Hand (Sou-Sou / Partner) Flow
Here’s what the processized version could look like in the cockpit:

Group Creation
One person creates the hand in CryptoOwnBank. They name it, set the contribution amount and frequency (e.g., $100 every Friday), set the total number of rounds, and invite others via link or QR. Everyone accepts with their own wallet/keys. The contract records the agreed schedule and rotation order.
Contribution Phase (Every Period)
On (or before) the due date, each member sees a clear prompt in their cockpit: “Your contribution for Round 3 of the Family Hand is due.” They approve and send from their own wallet. The cockpit shows who has paid and who hasn’t — visible to everyone in the group.
Payout Phase
When it’s someone’s turn, the contract automatically routes the accumulated pot to that person’s designated address (they set it when they joined or can update it). Everyone sees the payout happen on the shared ledger.
Tracker / Memory Layer (this is where the real daily/weekly value lives)
The cockpit shows each member:
Current round and whose hand it is
Who has paid this round (green checkmarks)
Full history of every contribution and payout
“Next action required” for them personally
Simple status like “You have received 2 hands, 6 remaining” or “You owe this week’s contribution”

End of Cycle
Once everyone has received their hand, the playbook can close or restart with the same (or edited) parameters.

This removes the classic failure points: forgotten turns, disputes about who paid, lost notebooks, and “I thought it was my week.”
Why This Fits the Bigger Vision

It directly extends the Legacy Plan mechanics you already have (multi-party consent, conditions, transparent flows, member-controlled keys).
It turns CryptoOwnBank into the coordination layer for sovereignty done together — not just individual self-custody.
Cookie-cutter playbooks mean a family can run a hand the same way another family (or church group, or diaspora network) runs one, without reinventing the process each time.
It stays true to “processize, don’t gate.” Anyone can create one. You just make the process clean and repeatable.
