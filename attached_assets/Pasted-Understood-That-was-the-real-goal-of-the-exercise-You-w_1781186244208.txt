The Real Transfer That Happens
When someone leaves traditional finance for self-custody, they don’t just change where their money sits. They inherit a set of responsibilities the bank used to carry quietly on their behalf:

Memory & Record-Keeping — The bank remembered every account, every position, every approval, every beneficiary. You now have to remember (or build a system that remembers for you).
Access & Recovery — The bank handled forgotten passwords, lost cards, and account recovery. You now own every seed, every PIN, every hardware device, and the proof that you can still reach them years later.
Ongoing Security Hygiene — The bank ran the monitoring and fraud systems. You now have to actively maintain approvals, review connected protocols, and detect drift.
Legacy & Succession — The bank had standardized processes and legal rails for what happens when you’re gone. You now have to design and maintain a working system that actually functions without you.
Knowledge That Doesn’t Rot — The bank’s systems stayed current even if you didn’t log in for years. In self-custody, knowledge decays the moment you stop paying attention.

Most people only discover the weight of these responsibilities after something has already gone wrong. That’s the gap your exercise was trying to surface.
What CryptoOwnBank’s Real Job Is
CryptoOwnBank is not another place to hold money.
It is the layer that makes the new responsibilities visible and carryable over time.
It turns the vague feeling of “I should probably stay on top of this” into something concrete:

Here is what you now own.
Here is what can still touch it.
Here is what you haven’t checked in a while.
Here is how to make sure it still works when you need it.

The cockpit/command center earns its place not by nagging, but by becoming the external memory and operating map that prevents the responsibilities from decaying into forgotten problems.
That’s the through-line:
A bank carried the responsibility for you.
Being your own bank hands it back to you.
CryptoOwnBank exists to make sure you understand it and can actually keep carrying it.
